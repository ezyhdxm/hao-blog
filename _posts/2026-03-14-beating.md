> When two pure tones are played together, something curious happens: the combined sound gets louder and softer in a regular pulse. This is called **beating**. Here we derive the mathematics and try it for ourselves.

<!--more-->

## What Happens When We Add Two Sine Waves?

A pure musical tone is a sine wave. If we play two tones at once — say, 438 Hz and 440 Hz — we are adding two sine waves. The air pressure at your ear is the sum of the two pressures. So the combined waveform is

$$
s(t) = A_1 \cos(2\pi f_1 t) + A_2 \cos(2\pi f_2 t),
$$

where \\(f_1\\) and \\(f_2\\) are the frequencies in Hertz, and \\(A_1\\), \\(A_2\\) are the amplitudes. For simplicity, we can take \\(A_1 = A_2 = 1\\) and work with

$$
s(t) = \cos(2\pi f_1 t) + \cos(2\pi f_2 t).
$$

The question is: what does this sum sound like?

---

## The Trigonometric Identity

The key is a standard identity for the sum of two cosines:

$$
\cos \alpha + \cos \beta = 2 \cos\left(\frac{\alpha - \beta}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{\alpha + \beta}{2}\right).
$$

Applying this with \\(\alpha = 2\pi f_1 t\\) and \\(\beta = 2\pi f_2 t\\):

$$
\cos(2\pi f_1 t) + \cos(2\pi f_2 t) = 2 \cos\left(2\pi \frac{f_1 - f_2}{2}\, t\right) \cos\left(2\pi \frac{f_1 + f_2}{2}\, t\right).
$$

So the sum factors into two cosines:

- **Carrier** \\(\cos(2\pi \frac{f_1 + f_2}{2} t)\\) — oscillates at the **average** frequency \\(\frac{f_1 + f_2}{2}\\).
- **Envelope** \\(\cos(2\pi \frac{f_1 - f_2}{2} t)\\) — oscillates at **half the difference** \\(\frac{\lvert f_1 - f_2 \rvert}{2}\\).

The envelope modulates the amplitude. When the envelope is \\(\pm 1\\), the combined wave is loud; when it crosses zero, the two waves cancel and the combined wave is quiet. So we hear a periodic pulsing — the **beat**.

---

## The Beat Frequency

The envelope is \\(\cos(\pi(f_1 - f_2)t)\\). The combined amplitude \\(2\lvert\cos(\pi(f_1-f_2)t)\rvert\\) reaches a maximum when the two waves are in phase (constructive interference) and drops to zero when they are out of phase (destructive interference).

Consecutive maxima occur when \\(\pi(f_1 - f_2)t = n\pi\\), i.e. \\(t = n/\lvert f_1 - f_2 \rvert\\). So the time between beats is \\(1/\lvert f_1 - f_2 \rvert\\) seconds. The **beat frequency** is therefore

$$
f_\text{beat} = \lvert f_1 - f_2 \rvert.
$$

If two tones differ by 2 Hz, you hear about 2 beats per second. When the frequencies match, the beating disappears.

## Why This Matters for Synth Building

Beating is not just a music trick. It is a direct consequence of adding waves together, and this idea appears everywhere in synthesis and signal processing. Detuning two oscillators slightly apart is a classic way to add thickness and movement to a sound. If you can hear and understand this interaction, you are already thinking like a synth designer.
